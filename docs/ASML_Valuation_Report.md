## 1. Company overview

**Business model.** ASML designs, assembles and services photolithography systems, the machines that print circuit patterns onto silicon wafers. It sells systems (FY25: €24.5bn [R]) and then earns recurring revenue from service contracts and field upgrades on its installed base (FY25: €8.2bn [R], 25% of sales).

**Products** (FY25 net system sales [R]):
| Technology | Units | € m |
|---|---:|---:|
| EUV (NXE) | 44 | 10,446 |
| EUV High-NA (EXE) | 4 | 1,157 |
| DUV ArF immersion | 131 | 10,311 |
| DUV ArF dry / KrF / i-line | 148 | 1,736 |
| Metrology & inspection | 208 | 825 |

**Revenue drivers.** Chipmakers' wafer-fab equipment budgets; leading-edge logic (66% of FY25 system sales) and memory (34%) node transitions; EUV layer count per wafer; price per tool (€200m+ for EUV, more for High-NA); installed-base growth.

**Geographic exposure** (FY25 sales by customer location [R]): China 29%, Taiwan 26%, South Korea 25%, United States 13%, Japan 4%, other 3%.

**Customer concentration** [R]: the largest customer was 23.9% of FY25 sales, the top two 38.0%, and four customers each above 10% made up 61.2% in total.

**Industry characteristics.** Highly cyclical capex end-market. A handful of buyers (TSMC, Samsung, Intel, SK hynix, Micron, Chinese fabs). Long lead times and order backlogs (€38.8bn at end-2025 [R]). Customer down payments fund ASML's working capital.

**Competitive advantages.** ASML is the sole EUV supplier after 20+ years and tens of billions in R&D. It controls critical suppliers (it acquired Cymer for the light source and has a close partnership with Zeiss optics). Customers co-funded EUV in 2012, which locks in the ecosystem. Switching costs are high, and service revenue grows with every installed tool.

**Main risks.** Semiconductor capex cycles; export controls on China (29% of sales); customer concentration; High-NA adoption timing; supply-chain capacity; EUR/USD.

**What drives the valuation.** Revenue growth after 2026, the gross/EBITDA margin trajectory, and the terminal assumptions (growth or multiple). Working capital matters for near-term FCF but much less for value.

*Operating NWC = (current assets - cash - short-term investments - current loans receivable) - (current liabilities - current debt) - non-current contract liabilities. A negative change = cash inflow.*

**Trends that matter for the forecast**
1. **Growth is lumpy.** A 15% four-year CAGR includes a near-flat FY24, so the forecast fades growth instead of extrapolating 30% years.
2. **Margins expand with EUV mix and scale.** EBITDA margin recovered from 33.5% (FY22) to 37.7% (FY25). R&D stays around 14-15% of sales, so operating leverage drives the forecast margin path.
3. **The tax rate is low and stable** at 15-19% (Dutch innovation box).
4. **Capex is light** (5-8% of sales) after the FY23-24 capacity build.
5. **Working capital is the volatility source.** FY23 FCF fell to €3.1bn because down payments were consumed, even though NOPAT rose. By June 2026 NWC had moved to about -7% of LTM sales after a €6.8bn H1 outflow [R].

**Justification**
* **Revenue.** FY26 = midpoint of the raised 2026 guidance of €43-45bn (Q2-26 release). FY27 +16% reflects a record backlog and AI-driven leading-edge capacity. Growth then fades to 5% by FY30 as the cycle normalises. FY30 revenue of €63bn sits just above ASML's 2030 range of €44-60bn (set at the 2024 Investor Day). That is justified because 2026 is already at the bottom of that range. CAGR FY25-30: 14.1%.
* **Margins.** The FY26 gross-margin guide is 54-56%. Base trends to 57%, inside the 2030 target of 56-60%. EBITDA margin rises 5pp from FY25 as revenue outgrows R&D and SG&A. Implied opex (R&D + SG&A) is about 17% of sales vs 18% in FY25, so the margin gain comes from operating leverage (checked automatically).
* **D&A and CapEx.** In line with history (D&A 2.5-3.3%, CapEx 5.0-8.0%). CapEx at 5.5% covers the continued EUV/High-NA capacity build.
* **Working capital.** NWC normalises to -12% of revenue, less negative than the FY21-25 average of -24%. This is conservative because down payments may not rebuild to past peaks. FY26 shows the outflow already visible in H1-26.
* **Tax.** 17.5%, in line with the FY24-25 effective tax rate. The Pillar Two 15% minimum is not binding.

## 10. Investment conclusion

**Base-case value: €1,103 per share, 31.6% below the €1,611.40 market price. On our assumptions the shares look overvalued.**

* **Primary drivers.** The terminal assumptions dominate: 76-87% of EV. Within the forecast, post-2026 revenue growth and the EBITDA margin matter most. A 1pp WACC change moves the blended value by about €75-95.
* **The disagreement is the insight.** The exit-multiple method (€1,430) sits only 11% below the market, while the perpetuity method (€776) is 52% below. Today's price needs either a ~29x terminal EBITDA multiple, in line with ASML's own five-year average, or growth that stays far above GDP after 2030. The market is effectively pricing ASML as a growth monopoly well into the 2030s.
* **Bull case drivers.** A sustained AI capex cycle, High-NA adoption earlier than planned, 60% gross margins and services scaling. That gives €1,718 (+7%).
* **Bear case drivers.** A 2027-28 capex digestion, wider China export restrictions and margin pressure. That gives €543 (-66%).
* **Key risks to this view.** If ASML keeps compounding at double digits beyond 2030, a 5-year DCF with a 2.5% terminal rate understates value. That is the main limitation of the method for this company.

This is a valuation exercise, not a recommendation. The model says the price embeds optimistic long-term assumptions, not that the business is weak.
