## 1. Company overview

**Business model.** ASML designs, assembles and services photolithography systems, the machines that print circuit patterns onto silicon wafers. It sells systems (FY25: €24.5bn [R]) and then earns recurring revenue from service and upgrades on its installed base (FY25: €8.2bn [R], 25% of sales).

**Products** (FY25 net system sales [R]):
| Technology | Units | € m | ASP (€ m) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| EUV low-NA (NXE) | 44 | 10,446 | 237 |
| EUV High-NA (EXE) | 4 | 1,157 | 289 |
| DUV immersion (ArFi) | 131 | 10,311 | 79 |
| DUV dry / KrF / i-line | 148 | 1,736 | 12 |
| Metrology & inspection | 208 | 825 | 4 |

**Revenue drivers.** Chipmakers' wafer-fab equipment budgets; leading-edge logic (66% of FY25 system sales) and memory (34%) node transitions; EUV layer count per wafer; system prices; growth of the installed base.

**Geographic exposure** (FY25 sales by customer location [R]): China 29%, Taiwan 26%, South Korea 25%, United States 13%, Japan 4%, other 3%.

**Customer concentration** [R]: the largest customer was 23.9% of FY25 sales, the top two 38.0%, and four customers above 10% each made up 61.2% in total.

**Industry characteristics.** Highly cyclical capex end-market. A handful of buyers (TSMC, Samsung, Intel, SK hynix, Micron, Chinese fabs). Long lead times and backlogs (€38.8bn at end-2025 [R]). Customer down payments fund ASML's working capital.

**Competitive advantages.** ASML is the sole EUV supplier after 20+ years of R&D. It controls critical suppliers (the Cymer light source, a partnership with Zeiss optics). Customers co-funded EUV in 2012. Switching costs are high, and service revenue grows with every installed tool.

**Main risks.** Semiconductor capex cycles; export controls on China; customer concentration; High-NA adoption timing; supply-chain capacity.

**What drives the valuation.** EUV shipments and pricing, the margin trajectory, how long the growth phase lasts (fade and terminal assumptions), and the discount rate.

*Operating NWC = (current assets - cash - short-term investments - current loans receivable) - (current liabilities - current debt) - non-current contract liabilities. A negative change = cash inflow.*

**Trends that matter for the forecast**
1. **Growth is lumpy.** A 15% four-year CAGR includes a near-flat FY24. That is why the forecast is built from units against stated capacity rather than extrapolated.
2. **Margins expand with EUV mix and scale:** EBITDA margin rose from 33.5% (FY22) to 37.7% (FY25), with R&D at about 14% of sales.
3. **Tax is low and stable** at 15-19% (Dutch innovation box). **Capex is light** at 5-8% of sales.
4. **Working capital drives FCF volatility.** FY23 FCF fell to €3.1bn as down payments were consumed. By June 2026 NWC had moved to about -7% of LTM sales after a €6.8bn H1 outflow [R].
5. **The market paid 26-40x year-end EBITDA** over FY21-25 (average 29.8x), the benchmark for the exit multiple.

## 5. DCF valuation

**Discounting.** The valuation date is 7 Oct 2026 and the balance sheet is from 28 Jun 2026. FY26 cash flow to discount = FY26E UFCF (€9,010m) minus H1-26 actual UFCF (-€1,291m) = €10,301m. Mid-year convention: discount periods are 0.12, 0.73, 1.73 ... 8.73 years. Terminal value is discounted from end FY35 (9.23 years).

**Exit multiple of 20x.** ASML averaged 29.8x year-end EBITDA in FY21-25 and peers trade at 42x trailing today. Both are multiples for a high-growth phase. By FY35 growth in the model has faded to 2.5%, so a mature-stage multiple a third below ASML's own average is appropriate (checked: below both benchmarks).

## 10. Investment conclusion

**Blended DCF value: €935 per share, 42% below the €1,611.40 market price. On intrinsic value the shares look overvalued; on relative value they are priced in line with the sector.**

* **Primary drivers.** EUV shipments and pricing, margins, and how long growth lasts. Even with a ten-year horizon, terminal value is 54-69% of EV. ±1pp WACC moves the blended value by about €80-100 (€853-1,037).
* **What the price implies.** A reverse DCF needs 33x FY35 EBITDA (above ASML's ~30x average in a high-growth decade) or 7.1% growth forever. The market is pricing ASML's growth phase as lasting far beyond 2035.
* **Bull case drivers.** Full use of the announced +30%/+30% capacity, faster High-NA adoption, and 60% gross margins. That gives €1,587, close to the market.
* **Bear case drivers.** A 2027-28 capex digestion, wider export restrictions and margin pressure. That gives €478.
* **Main limitation.** If AI-driven semiconductor demand compounds well into the 2030s, any finite-horizon DCF with a GDP-like terminal rate will undervalue ASML. The comps (€1,251-1,690) are the market's answer to that.

This is a valuation exercise, not a recommendation.
